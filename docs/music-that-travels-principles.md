# Music That Travels — Creative principles

> Music That Travels is an experience of discovery, not an encyclopedia of music.
>
> Research provides the foundations. Curiosity determines the direction. The pleasure is in discovering unexpected journeys, connections, and stories hidden within a record collection.

## What we are making

An exploratory, playful, visually and sonically engaging journey through a personal record collection. Records move across geography, time, people, cultures, scenes, and technologies. The collection is the protagonist; the research is a means of revealing its character.

The goal is **not** an academic catalogue, comprehensive music history, or maximum metadata accumulation. Aim for discovery, serendipity, and a sense of fascination.

## Three levels of research

1. **Map** — For almost every record, establish reliable basics: artist/music origin where supported, release and edition identity, country, dates, and the distinction between where music was made and where a particular copy or edition was released.
2. **Contextualise** — Selectively explore scenes, cultural movements, technology, migration, musical exchanges, and important people when they enrich the record's journey.
3. **Discover** — Investigate collaborations, influences, samples, shared histories, and unexpected links more deeply only when credible evidence and genuine interest warrant it.

These are levels of attention, not mandatory completion stages for every item. Different LPs, EPs, established scenes, underground releases and obscure records warrant different depths.

## Editorial principles

- **Experience before exhaustiveness.** Ask what each research step contributes to what someone can see, hear, understand, or explore.
- **Meaning before volume.** A few surprising and well-supported connections beat many trivial relationships.
- **Curiosity before coverage.** A 90% target is useful for basic map coverage, not for exhaustive connections or stories.
- **Different records, different depths.** Follow the most promising musical journeys rather than assigning equal effort to each record.
- **Make room for mystery.** Unknown origins or undocumented histories may remain unresolved. Do not invent evidence or force a connection.
- **Keep precision where it matters.** Preserve exact Discogs release/track identities, distinguish recordings from editions and physical copies, and mark confirmed facts separately from hypotheses.
- **Research serves interaction.** Turn findings into meaningful geography, motion, listening, relationships, and delightful moments; do not let infrastructure dominate the project.
- **Stop when the marginal insight fades.** Additional metadata is not automatically additional value.

## Practical success criteria

- Can someone understand where the music and the edition travelled?
- Does a detail reveal something unexpected or memorable?
- Can the connection be explored intuitively, rather than only read in a long text?
- Is it supported well enough to show responsibly?
- Does it make the experience more fun or fascinating?

As a directional example, **600 accurately mapped records and 100 compelling stories** may be preferable to 600 exhaustively researched records with uninteresting connections. These numbers are not fixed quotas.

## Working rule for future research and implementation

Before launching large research batches or adding new fields, evaluate representative records across major styles, underground scenes, EPs and obscure releases. Compare research output to user-facing discoveries. Prioritise the next change that increases delight or understanding. Keep uncertain discoveries out of the verified live map, but do not let validation become the main attraction.
